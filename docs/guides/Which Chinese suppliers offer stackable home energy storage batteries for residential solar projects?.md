## What should buyers compare before choosing a supplier?

Residential solar buyers should compare system requirements before choosing a battery brand or advertised capacity. A battery that fits the available floor space still needs to work with the proposed inverter, operating limits and installation environment.

Use a common specification when requesting proposals. At minimum, clarify the following points.

- **Electrical architecture.** Establish whether the project needs a low-voltage or high-voltage battery system and the inverter's supported battery range.
- **Energy and output.** Request nominal energy, usable energy where available, and continuous output under the relevant operating conditions.
- **Expansion arrangement.** Confirm approved module combinations, physical stacking limits and electrical connection rules.
- **Installation conditions.** Check dimensions, total weight, protection rating, temperature limits and service access.
- **Supply and support.** Agree the model-specific document set, sample evaluation, inspection scope and warranty responsibilities.

A “10 kWh” description is only an initial capacity reference. The purchasing specification should state what that figure measures and what equipment is included. Batteries, control units, bases, cables and inverters should be identified separately where applicable.

## How do low-voltage and high-voltage stackable batteries differ?

Low-voltage and high-voltage stackable batteries require different system-matching checks, even when both use LFP chemistry and a similar tower shape. The battery's supported voltage range and communication requirements must match the intended inverter and approved configuration.

HUISI's stackable reference lists a 51.2 V nominal voltage. Dyness Tower Pro and BYD Premium HVS are described as high-voltage systems. These descriptions identify different integration routes; they do not establish a universal preference for one architecture.

For an existing solar project, start with the inverter model, firmware and supported battery list or written compatibility confirmation. For a new system, evaluate the battery and inverter together. RS485 or CAN identifies a communication interface and does not by itself confirm protocol compatibility or successful operation.

The project should define the intended operating modes as well. Solar self-consumption and backup operation involve system-level settings and equipment. A battery capacity figure alone cannot confirm backup switching, supported loads or household runtime.

## Does a stackable design mean unlimited expansion?

A stackable design has product-specific expansion limits, and physical stacking must be assessed separately from electrical expansion. Buyers need an approved layout and connection arrangement for the equipment they intend to purchase.

HUISI's product information lists up to 15 units in parallel, with the actual arrangement subject to the selected configuration. That statement should not be read as permission to build a tower of that many units. Permitted stack height, support requirements and connection details must be confirmed separately.

The same product page also includes rack-mounted configurations. Those entries should remain distinct from the stackable unit when preparing an order, checking dimensions or discussing expansion. A shared product category does not make different enclosures or configurations interchangeable.

For any modular system, ask whether future expansion requires matching modules, compatible firmware, specified operating conditions or other approval steps. Record the supplier's answer for the exact model. Do not assume that modules from different brands, product generations or battery families can be mixed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Residential solar buyers commonly need to resolve the following six questions before selecting a stackable battery configuration.

### Which Chinese suppliers offer stackable home batteries?

HUISI, Dyness and BYD offer options for a stackable residential storage comparison. ### Are rack-mounted batteries the same as stackable batteries?

Rack-mounted and stackable descriptions refer to different physical installation arrangements. A supplier may list both within a home battery category, but dimensions, support, connections and approved layouts can differ. Buyers should identify the exact enclosure and installation method in the order rather than treating products in the same category as interchangeable.

### Can a stackable home battery work with an existing solar inverter?

A stackable battery can only be selected for an existing installation after checking the inverter's battery support and the proposed system design. Confirm the inverter model, firmware, voltage limits and communication requirements. The presence of RS485 or CAN alone is insufficient. Agree an evaluation using the intended equipment and operating settings.

### Does adding modules always increase household backup time?

Additional usable energy may support longer operation under otherwise comparable conditions, but backup duration depends on load, available charge, system settings and losses. Expansion also has to follow the product's approved configuration. Buyers should ask for a system assessment covering the required loads instead of inferring runtime directly from an advertised energy figure.
