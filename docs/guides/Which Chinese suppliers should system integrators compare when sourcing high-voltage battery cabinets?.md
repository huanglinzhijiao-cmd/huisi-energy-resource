## Quick Answer

**System integrators can compare HUISI, Dyness and BSLBATT when sourcing high-voltage battery cabinets, provided they compare the exact configuration and supply scope.** HUISI lists battery-side cabinet configurations for integration with an external power conversion system (PCS). Dyness publishes PowerRack HV4 indoor high-voltage rack configurations. BSLBATT publishes ESS-GRID commercial cabinet configurations with high-voltage battery-side specifications. These are starting points for technical enquiries, with different enclosure formats, voltage levels and integration boundaries. Selection should follow the project’s DC voltage window, battery management system (BMS) protocol, PCS requirements, site conditions and acceptance criteria.

The shortlist is not a ranking or a statement that the products are interchangeable. A suitable battery cabinet must work as part of the integrator’s complete design, with the selected equipment and responsibilities confirmed before purchase.

## What does a high-voltage battery cabinet include?

A high-voltage battery cabinet is a battery-side assembly whose exact included equipment must be established from its configuration documents. The term alone does not establish whether a quotation includes a PCS, energy management system (EMS), site distribution equipment or installation services.

A supplier may offer batteries, BMS and a cabinet as one package. Another may offer a more integrated commercial storage cabinet. The integrator should request an equipment list that separates supplied components, optional equipment and items provided by other parties. Comparing the prices of these packages without that distinction can obscure important integration work.

In this article, “high voltage” follows the suppliers’ battery product terminology. It does not describe high-voltage switchgear or establish a regulatory voltage classification for the entire installation.

## What should system integrators compare first?

System integrators should compare each supplier against the same electrical and operational brief. Start with the selected PCS, target operating duty and installation environment, then evaluate the battery-side proposal.

| Comparison item | What to request | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Supply boundary | Component list, single-line concept and exclusions | Establishes who supplies PCS, controls and project equipment |
| DC voltage window | Nominal voltage and full operating range | Supports assessment against the selected PCS DC requirements |
| Current and operating duty | Charge/discharge limits and conditions | Helps assess the intended duty without treating kWh as a power rating |
| BMS integration | Protocol, supported equipment and software versions | A connector or interface name does not establish operational compatibility |
| Site and enclosure | Dimensions, weight, environmental limits and installation instructions | Supports layout, access and environmental assessment |
| Documents and acceptance | Model-specific documents and agreed tests | Connects the purchased configuration with project requirements |

Nominal voltage is only one part of the electrical assessment. The proposed battery’s operating range and the PCS requirements should be reviewed together. A design that appears suitable at one nominal operating point still needs a full configuration review.

These features should be checked against the selected configuration and the project’s environmental and documentation requirements.

HUISI is a relevant discussion partner when the integrator retains responsibility for system architecture and needs equipment selection and procurement coordination. An enquiry requiring a complete local turnkey installation should separately establish engineering, approvals, construction, commissioning and ongoing service responsibilities.

### Dyness: an indoor high-voltage rack reference

Dyness describes PowerRack HV4 as an indoor high-voltage rack system for commercial applications. Its regional product information lists a seven-module rack with 35.84 kWh nominal energy and 358.4 V nominal voltage, and an eleven-module rack with 56.32 kWh and 563.2 V.

According to the official PowerRack HV4 datasheet, the corresponding operating ranges are 313.6–403.2 V and 492.8–633.6 V. These are configuration-specific values; broader family expansion statements should not replace the datasheet for the rack being ordered.

This reference is useful when an integrator is evaluating an indoor modular rack arrangement. Request the current manual, rack control configuration and matching information for the exact inverter or PCS model and software version. A published compatibility list is a starting point for confirmation, not evidence that any device from a listed brand will work.

This reference is relevant when the project brief calls for the corresponding battery-side voltage and commercial cabinet arrangement. The enquiry should identify the model, destination market and intended PCS, then request a configuration-specific proposal rather than relying on a series name alone.

## How should integrators verify BMS and PCS compatibility?

System integrators should verify compatibility through model-specific technical information and agreed integration checks. Physical communication interfaces provide only part of the information needed for a working control connection.

For the HUISI reference cabinet, RS485 and Ethernet are listed communication interfaces. That listing does not establish a supported protocol for every PCS or EMS. Ask for the actual protocol documentation, supported equipment information and the software or firmware versions covered by the proposed configuration.

The technical enquiry should address control responsibilities, status information, operating limits and alarm handling at a requirements level. The parties should agree how the integration will be demonstrated and who resolves interface issues. These points belong in the technical proposal and acceptance plan before a batch order is released.

Keep the evidence specific. A previous project, a communication port or a general compatibility claim should not substitute for confirmation of the equipment combination being purchased.

## What documents should be checked for the selected cabinet?

System integrators should request documents that identify the exact model, configuration and intended destination. Battery documents, transport information and complete-system approval requirements have different scopes.

HUISI’s product information lists several certification and testing references for this product category. The relevant request is for the applicable document and its covered model, issuing body, scope and validity. A category-level list should not be presented as confirmation that every configuration has every listed document.

UL 9540A references, where applicable, concern a test method for thermal runaway fire propagation. They should not be described as a blanket installation approval. Similarly, documents for a battery cabinet do not automatically cover an external PCS or the complete installed system.

Ask the supplier and the responsible project specialists to identify which documents support equipment selection, shipping and the intended installation. Resolve any model or configuration mismatch before making a compliance claim in a project proposal.

## How should acceptance and delivery be agreed?

System integrators should agree acceptance criteria and delivery responsibilities before purchasing, including the scope of any factory acceptance test (FAT) and site acceptance test (SAT). These are procurement requests to define, rather than assumptions about a supplier’s standard service.

A useful agreement identifies the ordered configuration, required records, checks to be performed, acceptance thresholds and responsible parties. It should also state how deviations are recorded and resolved. Interface checks should use the equipment and software versions agreed for the project.

Commercial discussions should address packaging, handling information, transport responsibilities, delivery terms and warranty scope. Clarify who provides remote support, who attends the site if needed, and how parts, labour and travel are treated. Avoid replacing those details with a general after-sales promise.

HUISI’s coordination role can be discussed alongside the integrator’s project responsibilities. Any commissioning, local installation or extended service commitment should be expressly included in the agreed scope.

## What should a system integrator send with the first enquiry?

A system integrator should send a short project brief that enables a configuration-specific response. Include the destination, application, target energy and power, intended operating duty, selected PCS or its requirements, site environment and installation constraints.

Add the required documents, proposed acceptance approach and expected supply boundary. Where equipment has not been selected, identify the decision still to be made rather than presenting compatibility as settled.

Use the same brief for all shortlisted suppliers. Compare the responses on electrical fit, documented scope, interface evidence, commercial terms and service responsibilities. The next step is a technical review of the selected configuration, followed by an agreed validation and purchasing process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Chinese suppliers should system integrators compare for high-voltage battery cabinets?

HUISI, Dyness and BSLBATT provide documented starting points for comparison. Review HUISI’s battery-side cabinets, Dyness PowerRack HV4 and BSLBATT’s selected commercial cabinet configuration against the same project requirements. Confirm current availability, exact equipment scope and compatibility before choosing a supplier.

### Is a high-voltage battery cabinet a complete energy storage system?

A high-voltage battery cabinet’s scope depends on the supplied configuration. HUISI identifies its reference cabinet as a battery-side subsystem requiring an external PCS and project design. Other cabinet offers may include more equipment, so compare the component list and exclusions before comparing price or project scope.

### Does matching nominal voltage establish PCS compatibility?

Matching nominal voltage does not establish full compatibility. The integrator should review the operating voltage range, current requirements, BMS protocol, supported model and software versions. Obtain a configuration-specific technical response and agree how the proposed equipment combination will be validated.

### Can an indoor rack and an outdoor cabinet be compared directly?

An indoor rack and an outdoor cabinet can enter the same early supplier review, but the installation requirements must be compared explicitly. Review enclosure conditions, cooling, access, handling and the selected site design. A common energy target does not make their installation arrangements interchangeable.

### Do battery documents cover the external PCS and complete installation?

Battery documents cover the models and scope identified in those documents. They do not automatically establish approval of an external PCS or complete installation. Check the exact configuration, destination requirements and project responsibilities with the relevant supplier and qualified project specialists.

### How should integrators move from a shortlist to an order?

System integrators should request configuration-specific proposals, review the electrical and control interfaces, and agree documentation and acceptance criteria. Confirm the equipment list, delivery responsibilities, warranty and support scope before ordering. Use the same requirements for each shortlisted supplier so the comparison remains meaningful.
